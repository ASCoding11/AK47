## 1. Are binary yes/no event contracts "swaps" / commodity options, and what does it mean for an unregistered platform to offer them to retail?

### Takeaway
Under the CFTC's long-standing and still-current position, a cash-settled yes/no event contract is a "swap" (an option and/or an event-contingent payment) and, before Dodd-Frank, an "option"; offering them to ordinary retail users (non-ECPs) is lawful only on a CFTC-designated contract market (DCM), and running a facility for them without DCM/SEF status is itself the violation. Since 9 Oct 2026 the CFTC has proposed to write "event contracts, including those based on sports-, politics-, cultural, and weather-related events" expressly into the swap definition, which removes any argument this founder's markets fall outside CFTC jurisdiction.

### Cited Findings
- Statutory definition: 7 U.S.C. 1a(47)(A) defines "swap" to include (i) any option "of any kind" for purchase or sale of, or based on the value of, one or more commodities, and (ii) any agreement "that provides for any purchase, sale, payment, or delivery ... that is dependent on the occurrence, nonoccurrence, or the extent of the occurrence of an event or contingency associated with a potential financial, economic, or commercial consequence." [statute, not re-fetched] — [7 U.S.C. 1a](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title7-section1a&num=0&edition=prelim)
- 7 U.S.C. 2(e): unlawful for any person other than an eligible contract participant (ECP) to enter into a swap unless the swap is entered into on, or subject to the rules of, a DCM. ECP status for individuals generally requires >$10M invested on a discretionary basis (>$5M if hedging) under 7 U.S.C. 1a(18). Retail users of a typical website are not ECPs. [statute, not re-fetched] — [7 U.S.C. 2](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title7-section2&num=0&edition=prelim)
- 7 U.S.C. 7b-3(a)(1) (CEA 5h(a)(1)): no person may operate a facility for the trading or processing of swaps unless registered as a SEF or a DCM. [statute, not re-fetched] — [7 U.S.C. 7b-3](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title7-section7b-3&num=0&edition=prelim)
- 7 U.S.C. 6(a) (CEA 4(a)) bars off-exchange futures; 7 U.S.C. 6c(b) (CEA 4c(b)) bars commodity option transactions contrary to CFTC rules (17 CFR Part 32 generally requires options to be swaps traded per CEA swap rules, subject to the "trade option" exemption for commercial parties, which retail bettors do not meet). [statute/reg, not re-fetched] — [7 U.S.C. 6c](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title7-section6c&num=0&edition=prelim); [17 CFR Part 32](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-32)
- CFTC's applied theory in Polymarket (2022): "binary options are a form of swaps under applicable law," so Polymarket could provide a facility for them only if registered as a DCM or SEF; it was charged with failing to obtain DCM designation or SEF registration. — [Baker McKenzie, Jan 5 2022](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2022/01/05/us-cftc-issues-1-4-million-fine-against-crypto-betting-service-polymarket-based-on-its-operating-swaps-without-proper-registration); [Nelson Mullins](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/the_vault/fn-in-the-news/blockratize-to-pay-usd1-4-million-penalty-to-cftc-for-operating-unregistered-swap-facility-and-non-designated-contract-market)
- Intrade (2012): CFTC enforcement director stated it is unlawful "to solicit U.S. persons to buy and sell commodity options, even if they are called 'prediction' contracts, unless they are listed and traded on a CFTC-registered exchange or legally exempt." — [CFTC Release 6423-12](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/6423-12)
- 9 Oct 2026: CFTC proposed rule (RIN 3038-AF82), "Further Definition of 'Swap'—Inclusions," would add "Event contracts, including those based on sports-, politics-, cultural, and weather-related events" to the (non-exhaustive) list of swap inclusions in 17 CFR Part 1; published in Federal Register 19 Oct 2026 (FR Doc. 2026-21377). — [CFTC Press Release 9310-26](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9310-26); [Federal Register 2026-21377](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/19/2026-21377/further-definition-of-swap-inclusions); [Skadden, Nov 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/11/cftc-moves-to-draw-the-line)
- Same day the CFTC adopted a companion interim final rule excluding casino-style gambling, including qualifying state-regulated sportsbook wagers, from the swap definition (leaving those to the states). — [cdcgaming.com](https://cdcgaming.com/cftc-proposes-rules-to-confirm-prediction-market-authority/); [gaming.net](https://www.gaming.net/cftc-proposes-to-define-sports-and-political-event-contracts-as-swaps/)
- Comment deadline reported as 18 Nov 2026; Gambling Insider reported only ~11 comments as the deadline neared (deadline single-sourced; verify). CFTC estimated avg monthly event-contract volume in Aug 2026 of about $1.5B, mostly sports. — [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/202711/cftc-sports-event-contract-rule-10-comments-deadline); [defiprime](https://defiprime.com/cftc-event-contracts-swap-proposal)
- Courts are split on whether sports event contracts are "swaps" (relevant mainly to state-preemption fights involving registered DCMs): Third Circuit (2-1, 6 Apr 2026) affirmed Kalshi's preliminary injunction against New Jersey; Ninth Circuit (3-0, 28 Aug 2026) ruled for Nevada, finding Kalshi's sports contracts likely not swaps (en banc petition pending); Sixth Circuit (25 Sept 2026, Ohio/Tennessee) held Kalshi had not shown its contracts are swaps and, even if they were, CEA would not preempt state law. New Jersey petitioned for certiorari 2 Sept 2026; Robinhood petitioned from the Ninth Circuit; as of ~11 Nov 2026 no cert grant; Kalshi response reportedly due 7 Dec 2026. — [lawcommentary.com](https://www.lawcommentary.com/articles/supreme-court-kalshi-state-sports-betting-laws); [predictionnews.news](https://predictionnews.news/supreme-court-preemption-analysis/); [igaming-times](https://igaming-times.com/news/prediction-markets/new-jersey-asks-the-supreme-court-to-decide-who-regulates-kalshi) (dates from secondary sources; verify on SCOTUS docket)

### Inferences
- The circuit split cuts both ways but does NOT help an unregistered operator. If event contracts are swaps (CFTC's view, Third Circuit, Oct 2026 proposal), the platform is an unregistered swap facility offering swaps to non-ECPs off-DCM (2(e), 5h). If some courts hold sports contracts are not swaps, they are then simply wagers subject to state gambling law (outside this researcher's scope), and the "federal preemption" shield that Kalshi relies on is available only to registered DCMs anyway. Either way, an unregistered P2P venue is exposed.
- Politics, economics and culture markets fit even more comfortably within 1a(47)(A)(ii) ("potential financial, economic, or commercial consequence") than sports; the Oct 2026 proposal names politics and culture explicitly.
- "Pays $1 to the winning side" = classic binary option/event contract structure that the CFTC has repeatedly treated as a swap/option (Intrade, Polymarket, Kalshi's own DCM-listed products).

## 2. Enforcement history: Intrade (2012), Polymarket/Blockratize (2022), later actions; Polymarket's 2025 QCEX acquisition, US relaunch, end of DOJ/CFTC probes

### Takeaway
The CFTC has twice taken action against unregistered prediction markets serving US users (Intrade 2012 -> $3M judgment in 2015; Polymarket 2022 -> $1.4M settled order and wind-down). Polymarket's route back into the US in 2025 was to buy an already-licensed DCM and clearinghouse (QCEX, ~$112M), not to argue it was "just a marketplace."

### Cited Findings
- Intrade: 26 Nov 2012 CFTC civil complaint (D.D.C.) against Ireland-based Intrade The Prediction Market Ltd and Trade Exchange Network Ltd (TEN) for soliciting and permitting US retail customers to trade off-exchange binary options, and for filing false "Annual Certification" forms claiming options were limited to eligible participants; TEN also charged with violating a 2005 CFTC order for similar conduct. Intrade cut off US customers within hours. — [CFTC Release 6423-12](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/6423-12); [complaint PDF](https://cftc.gov/idc/groups/public/%40lrenforcementactions/documents/legalpleading/enftradeexchangecomplaint11261.pdf); [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/features/intrade-tells-u-s-customers-to-empty-accounts-after-cftc-suit)
- Intrade outcome: summary judgment for CFTC on Counts I and II (31 July 2015); court ordered $3M civil monetary penalty, joint and several, finding US customers traded 5,503 binary option contracts (Sept 2007 to 25 June 2012). — [CFTC Release 7758-18](https://cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/7758-18); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/binary-options/brokers/remember-intrade-and-ten-cftc-hits-defunct-binary-options-brands-with-3m-fine/)
- Polymarket: 3 Jan 2022 order, In re Blockratize, Inc., CFTC Docket No. 22-09, filing and simultaneously settling charges for offering off-exchange event-based binary options; >900 separate event markets since ~June 2020; failure to obtain DCM designation or SEF registration; $1.4M civil monetary penalty (reduced for "substantial cooperation"); required to wind down non-compliant markets (by 14 Jan 2022), facilitate withdrawals, and cease and desist. — [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/cftc-issues-cease-and-desist-order-to-binary-options-operator-using-smart-contracts); [Baker McKenzie](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2022/01/05/us-cftc-issues-1-4-million-fine-against-crypto-betting-service-polymarket-based-on-its-operating-swaps-without-proper-registration); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/commoditiesderivativesstock-exchanges/1147658/facilitator-of-smart-contracts-protocol-fined-14-million-by-cftc-for-purportedly-enabling-unregistered-eventsbased-binary-options-trading-platform)
- Notably Polymarket was peer-to-peer/order-book-style (smart-contract markets where users traded with each other); the CFTC still treated the operator as running an unregistered facility. — [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/cftc-issues-cease-and-desist-order-to-binary-options-operator-using-smart-contracts)
- DOJ and CFTC closed their later investigations into Polymarket (which examined whether it accepted US users via VPNs despite the 2022 order); Polymarket notified early July 2025; reported by Bloomberg 15 July 2025; no charges; agencies did not comment publicly. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-15/polymarket-probe-ended-by-doj-in-win-for-crypto-bets-under-trump); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2025-07-15-us-justice-department-and-cftc-close-probes-into-polymarket-bloomberg-362703)
- July 2025: Polymarket acquired QCEX (QCX LLC, a CFTC-designated DCM, plus QC Clearing LLC, a DCO) for ~$112M. 3 Sept 2025: CFTC staff no-action letter (Release 9113-25) to QCX/QC Clearing granting relief from certain swap data reporting and recordkeeping requirements for event contracts; CEO called it a "green light." — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/cftc-no-action-polymarket-letter-qcex-event-contracts); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/337951/polymarket-green-light-prediction-markets-us-cftc); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/369377/polymarket-can-go-live-in-the-us-following-cftc-ruling-ceo-says)
- CRS (Mar 2026): Polymarket's main exchange is domiciled offshore and claims to block US users; offshore platforms cannot legally accept trades from US users, though reports indicate some do. — [CRS IF13187](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13187)
- 25 Feb 2026: CFTC advisory on insider trading in prediction markets, discussing two enforcement matters involving trading on Kalshi and stating a political candidate "potentially" violated Rule 180.1 by trading contracts on his own candidacy. — [CRS LSB11406](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11406)
- Earlier in Kalshi's sports launch (Feb 2025) CFTC staff asked Kalshi and Crypto.com to explain how Super Bowl contracts complied. — [Bloomberg, 3 Feb 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-03/crypto-com-kalshi-s-super-bowl-wagers-probed-by-us-derivatives-regulator)

### Inferences
- The enforcement template for an unregistered, US-facing event-contract site is well established: cease-and-desist, civil penalty, mandatory wind-down/refunds. The founder's facts (US retail, real money, binary $1 payout, multiple topics) match both Intrade and Polymarket.
- Current CFTC leadership is friendly to prediction markets, but its friendliness has been channeled through registration (DCM acquisitions, no-action relief to registered entities, rulemaking for DCMs). Closing Polymarket's probes in 2025 did not legalize unregistered operation; Polymarket's response was to buy a licence.
- Five-year statute of limitations (28 U.S.C. 2462) for civil penalties means a lenient 2025-26 posture does not eliminate risk from a future, less friendly Commission. [general law, not re-fetched]

## 3. Kalshi: DCM designation, KalshiEX v. CFTC, appeal dismissal, sports self-certification

### Takeaway
Kalshi shows the lawful path: become a DCM, self-certify contracts under Part 40, and litigate the CFTC's public-interest power. It won on election contracts (D.D.C. Sept 2024; CFTC dropped appeal May 2025) and launched sports contracts by self-certification in Jan 2025, which the CFTC never blocked.

### Cited Findings
- Kalshi (KalshiEX LLC) was designated a DCM by the CFTC in November 2020. [researcher knowledge; consistent with all sources describing Kalshi as a registered exchange] — [Jones Day case page](https://www.jonesday.com/en/practices/experience/2023/11/kalshi-wins-stay-of-cftc-ban-on-contracts-contingent-on-us-elections)
- Sept 2024: Judge Jia Cobb (D.D.C.) ruled for Kalshi, vacating the CFTC's 2023 order barring congressional-control contracts, finding Congress had not authorized the public-interest review that led to the ban; the D.C. Circuit declined to stay the ruling (Oct 2024). — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/kalshi-cftc-lawsuit-appeal-election-betting-contracts-drop-may-2025); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/319206/federal-court-clears-kalshi-to-list-election-betting-contracts-denies-cftcs-motion)
- CFTC moved to voluntarily dismiss its D.C. Circuit appeal on 5 May 2025 (each side bearing own costs); granted 7 May 2025, leaving the district court ruling in place. — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/05/08/cftc-kalshi-election-case-dismissed/); [Law360](https://law360.com/articles/2335808)
- Kalshi self-certified sports event contracts around 23-24 Jan 2025; under self-certification contracts go live unless the Commission stays/opens a review; CFTC never formally ruled on them. — [Vixio](https://vixio.com/insights/gc-kalshi-launch-sports-contracts-despite-cftc-review); [ingame.com](https://www.ingame.com/?p=44188); [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/241859/kalshi-self-certifies-more-nfl-prop-markets/)

### Inferences
- The legal "permission" Kalshi enjoys flows from DCM status (core principles, clearing through a DCO, customer-fund segregation, surveillance, self-certification under 17 CFR 40.2) - none of which an unregistered site has. Its wins limit the CFTC's power to ban contract categories on registered exchanges; they do not authorize off-exchange trading.

## 4. Rule 40.11 / CEA 5c(c)(5)(C), the 2024 proposal, and 2025-2026 CFTC posture

### Takeaway
The 2024 proposal to ban political/sports contracts was withdrawn on 4 Feb 2026 by Chairman Mike Selig; the CFTC then issued an ANPRM (Mar 2026), a June 2026 NPRM replacing Rule 40.11's categorical approach with case-by-case "public interest determinations" and a definition of "gaming," and an Oct 2026 NPRM putting event contracts expressly in the swap definition. All of this governs what registered DCMs may list; none of it creates a path for unregistered venues.

### Cited Findings
- CEA 5c(c)(5)(C) (7 U.S.C. 7a-2(c)(5)(C)) lets the Commission prohibit listing of event contracts involving activity unlawful under federal or state law, terrorism, assassination, war, gaming, or similar activity it determines contrary to the public interest; Rule 40.11 (17 CFR 40.11) bars DCMs from listing such contracts and provides a 90-day review. [statute/reg, not re-fetched] — [17 CFR 40.11](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-40/section-40.11); [CRS LSB11441](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/LSB11441.html)
- 2024 proposal (dated 10 June 2024) would have restricted political and sports-related event contracts; CFTC withdrew it and said it does not intend to finalize it, effective 4 Feb 2026; Selig also withdrew a 2025 staff advisory on sports event contracts that he said "inadvertently created confusion." — [Corporate Compliance Insights](https://www.corporatecomplianceinsights.com/cftc-withdraws-proposed-rule-prediction-markets/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/cftc-withdraws-proposal-ban-sports-political-prediction-markets); [Sidley, Feb 2026](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/02/us-cftc-signals-imminent-rulemaking-on-prediction-markets); [FR withdrawal 2026-02454 summary](https://policyrisk.com/research/federal-register/2026-02454)
- Leadership: Caroline Pham was acting chair from Jan 2025; Michael Selig was confirmed 18 Dec 2025 and sworn in as 16th CFTC chairman 22 Dec 2025; Pham departed. Selig has filed/supported an amicus position (Nevada) that prediction markets are within exclusive federal jurisdiction. — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/michael-selig-sworn-in-as-cftc-chairman-what-to-know-now/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/cftc-changes-guard-selig-takes-reins-pham-departs); [The Block, 12 Jan 2026](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-01-12-cftc-chair-selig-innovation-committee-agency-weighs-crypto-prediction-markets-385156)

## 5. PredictIt / Victoria University no-action letter, Clarke v. CFTC, and whether a small operator could get one

### Takeaway
PredictIt operated under a 2014 staff no-action letter with tight conditions (non-profit academic, capped traders and position size); the CFTC withdrew it in Aug 2022, the Fifth Circuit blocked the shutdown (2023-2024), and the case settled in 2025 with a revised no-action letter. A no-action letter is discretionary staff forbearance, not a licence, and a for-profit startup taking 10% of winnings is a poor candidate.

### Cited Findings
- 2014 no-action letter (CFTC Letter 14-130, to Victoria University of Wellington) was revoked in 2022, with DMO directing PredictIt to cease operations by 15 Feb 2023. — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/predictit-and-cftc-reach-settlement-in-longstanding-lawsuit); [Willkie](https://complianceconcourse.willkie.com/articles/court-to-cftc-take-no-action-on-predictit-event-contract-no-action-relief/)
- Clarke v. CFTC (5th Cir.): court blocked the shutdown, holding the rescission likely arbitrary and capricious and treating the withdrawal as reviewable — a split with other circuits on finality of staff letters. — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/fifth-circuit-blocks-government-shutdown-of-political-gambling-market/); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/how-betting-platform-predictits-legal-struggle-could-hamper-regulators-and-hurt-regulated-firms); [Sidley/Law360 PDF](https://www.sidley.com/-/media/uploads/regulatory-litigation/law360--5th-circ-ruling-will-spur-challenges-to-noaction-letters.pdf)
- June-July 2025: CFTC and PredictIt settled; PredictIt announced (16 July 2025) a revised no-action agreement allowing it to "significantly expand" operations; its parent Aristotle has sought DCM status since ~2021 (approval unconfirmed). — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/predictit-and-cftc-reach-settlement-in-longstanding-lawsuit); [Nat'l Law Review press release](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/predictit-announces-resolution-litigation-cftc-and-bright-future-ahead); [Next Event Horizon](https://nexteventhorizon.substack.com/p/predictit-gets-upgrades)
- Original 2014 conditions (from researcher knowledge, not re-fetched): non-profit, academic research purpose; max 5,000 traders per contract; $850 per-participant investment cap per contract. — [Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/publication-listing/elections-betting-pays-off-for-now-implications-for-future-event-contracts)
- No-action letters are issued by CFTC staff under 17 CFR 140.99, bind only the issuing staff division (not the Commission or DOJ), apply only to the named requester and facts, and can be withdrawn. [reg, not re-fetched] — [17 CFR 140.99](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-140/subpart-D/section-140.99)

- Realistic for this founder? Unlikely: PredictIt's letter rested on academic, non-profit, capped-size features; the 2025 relief went to a long-established operator in litigation and to a licensed DCM (QCX). Under the current Commission, the practical route is DCM designation (or partnering with/listing on an existing DCM, or acquiring one), not stand-alone no-action relief.

## 6. Does being a no-position, pure matching venue reduce exposure? Who is liable; penalties and criminal exposure

### Takeaway
No. Being a neutral order-book venue is exactly what makes the platform an unregistered "facility" (5h) / "board of trade" — the violation is operating it, not taking positions. The entity, its officers/controlling persons, and aiders/abettors are exposed to civil penalties and, if willful, criminal prosecution.

### Cited Findings
- Polymarket and Intrade were both venues where customers traded against each other; both were charged with offering/operating unregistered markets. — [CFTC Release 6423-12](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/6423-12); [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/cftc-issues-cease-and-desist-order-to-binary-options-operator-using-smart-contracts)
- Liability provisions [statute, not re-fetched]: 7 U.S.C. 13c(a) (aiding/abetting, counseling, inducing a CEA violation = liable as principal); 7 U.S.C. 13c(b) (controlling-person liability, unless the controller acted in good faith and did not knowingly induce); 7 U.S.C. 2(a)(1)(B) (acts of agents/officers attributed to the firm). — [7 U.S.C. 13c](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title7-section13c&num=0&edition=prelim)
- Remedies [statute, not re-fetched]: administrative cease-and-desist and civil monetary penalties (7 U.S.C. 9, 13a); injunctions, restitution, disgorgement, and per-violation penalties of the greater of an inflation-adjusted statutory amount or triple the monetary gain in federal court (7 U.S.C. 13a-1(d)); inflation-adjusted figures in 17 CFR 143.8. — [7 U.S.C. 13a-1](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title7-section13a-1&num=0&edition=prelim); [17 CFR 143.8](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-143/subpart-A/section-143.8)
- Criminal [statute, not re-fetched]: 7 U.S.C. 13(a)(5) makes it a felony to willfully violate any CEA provision or rule (fine up to $1M and/or up to 10 years). — [7 U.S.C. 13](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title7-section13&num=0&edition=prelim)
- Real-world magnitudes: Polymarket $1.4M (settled, with cooperation credit); Intrade $3M (litigated). — [Baker McKenzie](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2022/01/05/us-cftc-issues-1-4-million-fine-against-crypto-betting-service-polymarket-based-on-its-operating-swaps-without-proper-registration); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/binary-options/brokers/remember-intrade-and-ten-cftc-hits-defunct-binary-options-brands-with-3m-fine/)
- DOJ did investigate Polymarket (2024-25), showing criminal-side interest in a platform suspected of serving US users after a CFTC order. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2025-07-15-us-justice-department-and-cftc-close-probes-into-polymarket-bloomberg-362703)

### Inferences
- "Just a marketplace" framing is legally backwards under the CEA: the marketplace is the regulated thing. Calling contracts "predictions" didn't help Intrade (CFTC's quote above). The 10% fee on winners strengthens the commercial-operator picture and supports "triple the monetary gain" penalty calculations.
- Willfulness for criminal purposes is easier to prove once the founder has documented awareness (e.g., this very legal analysis, or structuring choices made "to avoid liability"). A founder who proceeds after being advised the activity requires registration is in a worse position than Polymarket's 2020 founders.
- User-created markets add exposure: users may create Rule 40.11-type contracts (assassination, war, terrorism) or markets they can influence (manipulation/insider trading under 7 U.S.C. 9(1) and Rule 180.1).

### Gaps
- Current inflation-adjusted per-violation penalty amounts not retrieved.
- No source located on individual (founder) liability in the Polymarket order specifically; it was a settlement with the entity only (as reported).

## 7. FCM / introducing broker / commodity pool: holding customer funds in escrow

### Takeaway
Yes, likely additional exposure: an entity that solicits or accepts orders for swaps/futures/options and accepts money to margin or secure them is a futures commission merchant; holding both sides' stakes in escrow pending settlement fits that, and the CFTC has charged unregistered FCM violations against retail binary-options operators. On a DCM model those functions are split among the DCM, a registered DCO, and (if intermediated) FCMs with segregated customer accounts.

### Cited Findings
- Statutory FCM definition, 7 U.S.C. 1a(28): a person engaged in soliciting or accepting orders for futures, swaps, or commodity options (among others) and who, in connection, accepts money, securities or property to margin, guarantee or secure resulting trades. Registration required by 7 U.S.C. 6d; customer funds must be segregated (6d(a)(2), 6d(f) for cleared swaps). [statute, not re-fetched] — [7 U.S.C. 1a](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title7-section1a&num=0&edition=prelim); [7 U.S.C. 6d](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title7-section6d&num=0&edition=prelim)
- Polymarket's US route included acquiring a clearinghouse (QC Clearing, a DCO) alongside the DCM, reflecting that holding and settling customer collateral is itself a regulated function. — [Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2025/09/04/polymarket-green-light-from-cftc-and-acquisition-of-qcex-for-112-million-the-relaunch-in-the-usa/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/cftc-no-action-polymarket-letter-qcex-event-contracts)

### Inferences
- Kalshi-style DCMs operate "direct to customer" with a clearing affiliate holding funds; an unregistered site doing the same combines unregistered exchange + unregistered clearing/FCM activity.
- Commodity pool operator status is unlikely (the platform does not pool funds for collective trading), and introducing broker status is subsumed by the FCM/exchange analysis. Funds-holding also implicates money-transmission law (covered by another researcher).

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from retrieved sources whether the Polymarket and Intrade charges included unregistered FCM counts (researcher's recollection: Polymarket order charged 5h(a)(1)/4c(b)-type violations, not FCM; earlier retail binary-options cases such as Banc de Binary (2013) did include unregistered FCM charges — unverified here).

## 8. Does play money (no real-money deposits, withdrawals or prizes) take it outside the CEA?

### Takeaway
Very likely yes, in practice: CEA jurisdiction attaches to transactions in swaps, options and futures, i.e., agreements providing for a payment or delivery of value; pure play money with no purchase, cash-out or prizes involves no "payment" and the CFTC has not pursued such sites. But any convertibility (paid tokens, sweepstakes cash, crypto, gift-card prizes) can bring it back in.

### Cited Findings
- The swap definition turns on an agreement "that provides for any purchase, sale, payment, or delivery" contingent on an event [statute, not re-fetched]; every CFTC event-contract enforcement action identified (Intrade, Polymarket) involved real-money or crypto deposits and payouts. — [7 U.S.C. 1a](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title7-section1a&num=0&edition=prelim); [CFTC Release 6423-12](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/6423-12); [Baker McKenzie](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2022/01/05/us-cftc-issues-1-4-million-fine-against-crypto-betting-service-polymarket-based-on-its-operating-swaps-without-proper-registration)

### Inferences
- Play money removes the "payment" and the customer-funds issues and therefore the core 2(e)/5h/FCM exposure. Keep it strictly non-redeemable (no purchasable currency convertible to value, no prizes, no tradable tokens). A "sweepstakes" or crypto-token hybrid would likely be analyzed as real-money trading.
- Other options that change the analysis: (a) obtain DCM designation (plus DCO or clearing arrangement) — the Kalshi route; (b) acquire a DCM — the Polymarket/QCEX route; (c) become an introducing/referral partner or front-end for a registered DCM (needs separate analysis of IB/FCM registration); (d) restrict to ECPs only (impractical for retail); (e) exclude US persons entirely with real geoblocking — Polymarket's 2022-2025 posture, which still drew DOJ/CFTC scrutiny over VPN leakage.

### Gaps
- Found no CFTC statement, staff letter or case squarely holding that play-money prediction markets are outside the CEA; this conclusion is inference from the statutory text and enforcement pattern. No source located on whether paid "premium currency" that cannot be cashed out has been addressed by the CFTC.
