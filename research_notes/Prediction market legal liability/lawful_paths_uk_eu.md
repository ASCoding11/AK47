## 1. Becoming a CFTC Designated Contract Market (DCM) and clearing via a DCO

### Takeaway
Building your own DCM (17 CFR Part 38) plus a DCO (Part 39), or contracting one for clearing, is the most legally robust US route. It is also out of reach for a small self-funded founder. Industry estimates put it at 12 to 24+ months and about $5M+ in financial resources before legal and compliance staff, and the incumbents mostly bought licensed shells instead (Polymarket paid $112M for QCEX). User-created markets are possible only in a limited, exchange-curated form, because the DCM must self-certify each contract or contract class under §40.2. The July 2026 CFTC staff advisory narrowed broad "template" certifications.

### Cited Findings
- The CFTC designated KalshiEX LLC as a DCM in 2020 (press release 8302-20). Secondary sources date it to November 2020, and one says 2021; the CFTC release is the best support for 2020. — [CFTC Release 8302-20](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8302-20)
- Kalshi's clearing affiliate, Kalshi Klear LLC, received DCO registration on 29 Aug 2024, about four years after DCM designation. — [Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/cftc-grants-kalshi-klear-llc-dco-registration-2024829)
- KPMG (2026) estimates DCM registration at **12–24+ months**. The statutory 180-day review clock starts only once the application is "materially complete." Financial-resources requirements are **typically $5M+**. DCO registration is typically pursued alongside a DCM by firms seeking vertical integration. This is an estimate, not a CFTC figure. — [KPMG, Prediction markets: paths to entry (2026)](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/prediction-markets-path-entry.html) ([PDF](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2026/prediction-markets-paths-to-entry.pdf))
- The regulatory framework is 17 CFR Part 38 (DCM core principles, implementing CEA §5(d)'s 23 core principles) and 17 CFR Part 39 (DCOs). — [eCFR Part 38](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-38); [eCFR Part 39](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-39) (not fetched this session; cited as the primary text)
- No reliable public figure was found for what Kalshi spent on legal and compliance work to obtain designation (see Gaps).
- **Acquisition shortcut, reported deals:**
  - Polymarket agreed in July 2025 to buy QCEX (QCX LLC, a DCM designated 9 Jul 2025, plus QC Clearing LLC, a DCO) for **$112M**. — [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/polymarket-qcex-acquisition); [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/07/21/polymarket-buys-qcex-targets-us-return/)
  - The CFTC then issued an **Amended Order of Designation (24–25 Nov 2025)** that lets Polymarket US offer event contracts through CFTC-registered intermediaries (FCMs and brokers). A limited US app launched around 3 Dec 2025. — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/polymarket-receives-cftc-approval-of-amended-order-of-designation-enabling-intermediated-us-market-access-302625833.html); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/the-cftc-s-polymarket-decision-bringing-1443992/); [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/markets/polymarket-rolls-out-us-app)
  - Polymarket filed for FCM status on 3 Jul 2026 to offer margin trading; the outcome was not confirmed. — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/polymarket-us-margin-trading-approval/)
  - Robinhood and Susquehanna agreed on 25 Nov 2025 to buy 90% of MIAXdx (formerly LedgerX: a DCM, DCO and SEF). Terms were undisclosed and MIAX keeps 10%. MIAX had bought LedgerX for $50M in 2023 out of FTX's bankruptcy. — [Cointelegraph/Reuters](https://cointelegraph.com/news/robinhood-prediction-markets-ftx-linked-acquisition-report); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/robinhood-acquires-ledgerx-to-expand-prediction-markets-amid-overwhelming-growth-nov-26-2025)
  - DraftKings completed its acquisition of the CFTC-licensed Railbird Exchange on 21 Oct 2025 (price undisclosed) for "DraftKings Predictions." Polymarket Clearing was reported as its designated clearinghouse. — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/10/21/draftkins-railbird-prediction-market/); [CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2025/10/22/draftkings-railbird-acquisition-prediction-markets/)
  - FanDuel and CME Group announced a joint-venture FCM platform in August 2025. — [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/draftkings-railbird-acquisition-prediction-markets/)
  - Other licensed DCMs keep appearing, such as the DRW-linked Optex Markets. A startup DCM applicant, DimeTrades, filed using Kalshi's former attorneys. — [DeFi Rate (Optex)](https://defirate.com/?p=15843); [DeFi Rate (DimeTrades)](https://defirate.com/?p=6130)
  - ProphetX, formerly a state-licensed exchange, obtained CFTC approval to run a prediction market and raised $35M in July 2026. — [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/166600/prophetx-gets-cftc-approval-to-operate-prediction-market); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/prophetx-raises-35m-to-scale-its-sports-prediction-market-platform-july-28-2026)
- **Contract listing, and whether users can create markets:**
  - Under §40.2 a DCM may list a contract without prior approval by filing a written self-certification by close of business on the business day before listing. The DCM keeps primary responsibility for ongoing compliance. — [CFTC Listing Procedures](https://www.cftc.gov/IndustryOversight/ContractsProducts/ListingProcedures/index.htm) (page may be dated; verify against eCFR)
  - On 24 Jul 2026 the CFTC's Division of Market Oversight issued Advisory 26-22. It says broad "template-style" certifications covering many variations should **not** be filed under §40.2(a), and should instead go under §40.2(d) class certification or §40.3 approval. A §40.2(d) series must reference a prior, specific contract that the DCM itself filed. — [CFTC Release 9273-26](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9273-26); [CFTC Letter 26-22](https://www.cftc.gov/csl/26-22/download); [Markets Media](https://www.marketsmedia.com/cftc-advises-on-self-certification-of-event-contract-series/)
- **Legal environment, which is still unsettled:**
  - The CFTC issued an ANPRM on prediction markets (comments due 30 Apr 2026). On 19 Oct 2026 it proposed defining sports, politics, culture and weather event contracts as swaps, together with an interim final rule saying sportsbook and casino bets are not swaps. — [CRS LSB11441](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11441); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/ad8a494a/prediction-markets-at-a-crossroads-preemption-enforcement-and-rulemaking)
  - The courts are split:
    - The Third Circuit (6 Apr 2026, 2–1, preliminary injunction posture) held that CFTC jurisdiction over sports event contracts is likely exclusive as against New Jersey. — [3d Cir. opinion](https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/251922p.pdf); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/federal-appeals-court-cftc-jurisdiction-over-sports-event-contracts)
    - A Sixth Circuit panel reportedly held that states (Ohio, Tennessee) may apply gambling law. This was not verified against the opinion. — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/ad8a494a/prediction-markets-at-a-crossroads-preemption-enforcement-and-rulemaking)
    - In SDNY (Novig), Judge McMahon held that sports contracts are sports betting and called a CFTC order protecting Kalshi a "collusive effort." Novig has appealed. — [Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/igaming/judge-says-cftcs-kalshi-order-smacks-collusive-effort-novig-ruling/)
    - In Minnesota, a federal court found the state statute likely at least partially preempted. — [D. Minn. order (Jul 2026)](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/kalshi-ellison-walz-anglin-minnesota-prelim-inj-order.pdf)

### Inferences
- A de novo DCM plus DCO is realistically a $5M–$10M+ undertaking, counting capital, legal fees, a CCO and staff, surveillance technology, and 12–24+ months. This is an inference from KPMG's $5M+ resources figure plus personnel, not a reported total. Acquisition prices ($50M for LedgerX in 2023, $112M for QCEX in 2025) show what licences cost on the market, and both are far beyond a self-funded founder.
- Fully user-created markets that go live instantly fit poorly with a DCM. Each new contract or series needs a DCM self-certification that the DCM is responsible for, and Advisory 26-22 discourages open-ended templates. A feasible design is "user proposes, exchange reviews, certifies and lists," or class certifications for narrow, closely related series (for example, price thresholds on a single reference source).
- A take of 10% of winners' profit could be built as an exchange fee schedule on a DCM. Fee schedules are DCM rules subject to certification.

### Gaps
- No primary or reputable report of Kalshi's actual legal and compliance spend to obtain DCM designation, or of the date it filed. The "~2 years" figure in the brief was not independently confirmed.
- No CFTC application fee for DCM or DCO designation was found.
- Status of the 19 Oct 2026 proposed rule, the Sixth Circuit opinion text, and any Supreme Court petition is unverified.

## 2. Partnering: Introducing Broker or front end on a registered DCM

### Takeaway
The realistic regulated "lite" path is to become an NFA-registered Introducing Broker, or a "guaranteed IB" backed by an FCM, that routes customers to Kalshi or Polymarket US. The founder keeps the UI, brand and community, and earns referral or fee-share economics. The founder gives up the order book, contract creation and custody of funds. Big brokers (Robinhood, Webull, Coinbase, Moomoo) do this as FCMs. Sports apps like DraftKings and Fanatics reportedly use the IB-plus-third-party-FCM model.

### Cited Findings
- Robinhood launched a prediction markets hub in March 2025 through Robinhood Derivatives (an FCM), offering Kalshi and ForecastEx contracts. Webull partnered with Kalshi in 2025, later adding hourly BTC/ETH contracts. Coinbase rolled out Kalshi markets to US users. — [DeFi Rate "Land Grab"](https://defirate.com/news/prediction-market-land-grab-dcms-fcms-and-the-race-to-control-the-stack/); [Cointelegraph (Webull)](https://cointelegraph.com/news/webull-brokerage-kalshi-contracts-platform); [Casino.org (Coinbase)](https://www.casino.org/news/coinbase-latest-big-brokerage-name-to-enter-prediction-markets/)
- Moomoo Financial was approved by NFA as an FCM to support event contracts. — [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/moomoo-joins-retail-brokerage-push-into-prediction-markets/)
- An IB introduces customer orders but does not hold customer funds. DraftKings and Fanatics reportedly use an IB model paired with a third-party FCM. — [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/prediction-market-land-grab-dcms-fcms-and-the-race-to-control-the-stack/)
- Kalshi's CEO reportedly said close to 20 brokers were in the pipeline. The public rollout has been slower. — [InvestmentNews](https://investmentnews.com/emerging-markets/schwab-monitors-prediction-markets-as-kalshi-targets-brokerages/260787)
- Polymarket's Nov 2025 amended order specifically enables intermediated access via FCMs and brokerages. — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/cftc-expands-polymarkets-designation-to-allow-intermediated-trading-in-us/)
- **Independent IB capital:** minimum adjusted net capital is the greater of $45,000, $6,000 per office, or $3,000 per sponsored AP. NFA's compliance page says it expects IBs to maintain at least $50,000. — [NFA Independent IB Requirements](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/ib/regulatory-obligations/independent-ib-financial-requirements.html); [NFA IB Applicant Compliance Requirements](https://www.nfa.futures.org/registration-membership/who-has-to-register/ib-applicants-compliance-requirements.html); [CFTC min. net capital](https://www.cftc.gov/IndustryOversight/Intermediaries/FCMs/fcmibminimumnetcapital.html)
- **Guaranteed IB:** files a guarantee agreement with an FCM and is not subject to the minimum net capital or financial reporting requirements. It generally must introduce all its business to that FCM. — [NFA Guaranteed IB Requirements](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/ib/regulatory-obligations/guaranteed-ib-requirements.html)
- Associated persons must register and pass the Series 3 exam, and the firm needs written supervisory, AML and other procedures. This comes from a secondary source. — [terms.law IB guide](https://terms.law/Trading-Legal/guides/introducing-broker-agreements.html)
- KPMG lists NFA-registered intermediary routes with much shorter timelines than a DCM. A search summary cited "3–6 months / 60–90 days," but which category those figures belong to could not be confirmed. — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/prediction-markets-path-entry.html)

### Inferences
- **What the founder could keep:** branding, UI, community and social features, research content, and possibly a "suggest a market" funnel that the partner DCM reviews and certifies. The 10% take-rate model would become a negotiated commission or rebate per contract (not a cut of winnings), subject to the partner and FCM agreements.
- **What the founder loses:** own order book and matching, unilateral market creation, custody, and direct control over fees and KYC.
- A guaranteed IB under an FCM willing to take on a startup is the cheapest regulated US real-money route. A plausible estimate is low six figures and 3–9 months (legal, Series 3, compliance, NFA dues), but this is an estimate, not a reported figure. Finding a willing FCM or DCM partner is the binding constraint.
- Sports contracts carry state-law litigation risk even for intermediaries (see §1 courts). State gaming regulators have threatened sportsbook licensees who participate.

### Gaps
- Current NFA IB registration and annual dues amounts were not retrieved (NFA fee schedule not fetched). The brief's "$20,000" figure was not confirmed.
- No public terms of Kalshi or Polymarket broker revenue-share agreements were found.
- No evidence was found of any DCM letting third-party front ends originate new markets.

## 3. Play-money and free-to-play (Manifold model)

### Takeaway
A pure play-money market with no cash-out and no prize value is the cheapest and lowest-risk legal path in all states. Manifold proves the model at scale. It ran a real-money "Sweepcash" layer from Sept 2024 to 28 Mar 2025, then shut it down and returned to mana-only.

### Cited Findings
- Manifold ran Sweepcash, a redeemable sweepstakes coin, from about September 2024 to 28 March 2025, then ended it. The platform is now mana-only, mana has no cash value or cash-out path, and the only off-platform use is donating to charity at M100 per $1. Some secondary sources say April 2025; all are third-party reviews. — [Tech-Insider review](https://tech-insider.org/prediction-markets/platforms/manifold-markets-review/); [Wikipedia mirror](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Manifold_(prediction_market))
- Users can buy mana with real money, which is a purchase of play currency with no cash prize. — [FantasyLabs](https://www.fantasylabs.com/?p=190629)
- Commentary argues that choosing mana over cash settlement removed Manifold from the scope of state gambling regulators and the CFTC. This is commentary, not legal opinion. — [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/manifold-markets-review/)
- Gambling generally requires three elements: consideration, chance and prize. Removing any one avoids gambling, and the skill tests vary by state (dominant factor, material element, any chance). — [Ifrah Law](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/gambling-on-skill-enforcement-of-gambling-laws-against-skill-games-in-outlier-states/); [Lewis Roca guide](https://lewisroca.com/a-practical-guide-for-offering-online-contests-and-promotions-1)

### Inferences
- **Lawful in all 50 states, if strictly no prize:** no cash-out, no redeemable or transferable currency, no prizes of value tied to balances, and no secondary market for accounts or tokens. Charity donation of play money is a gray area that Manifold accepts; it is lower risk if it is funded by the company rather than redeemed per user.
- Selling play currency is lawful when it can never be converted to anything of value. Adding any redemption path recreates a gambling or sweepstakes problem (see §4).
- Monetization options: ads, subscriptions or premium features, B2B forecasting (private markets for companies), and selling data or API access.
- Consumer-protection, privacy and app-store rules still apply.

### Gaps
- No official Manifold statement on its legal rationale for ending Sweepcash was found.

## 4. Sweepstakes / dual-currency model

### Takeaway
High and rising risk. California (AB 831, effective 1 Jan 2026) and New York (SB 5935, signed 5 Dec 2025) banned dual-currency sweepstakes casinos. Connecticut, Montana and New Jersey also enacted bans, and one tracker counts about 11 states with statutory bans. California's law extends liability to vendors. Manifold and ProphetX both moved away from sweepstakes models.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanism: free "gold coins" for play, plus a promotional "sweeps coin," given free via AMOE or bundled with coin purchases, that can be redeemed for cash. — [Nelson Mullins](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/cards-on-the-table/all/game-over-states-mull-sweepstakes-casino-bans)
- California: AB 831 was signed 11 Oct 2025 and took effect 1 Jan 2026. It bans online sweepstakes casinos and expands liability to vendors and suppliers. — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/); [iGB](https://igamingbusiness.com/gaming/gaming-regulation/newsom-california-sweepstakes-casinos-ban/)
- New York: SB 5935 was signed 5 Dec 2025. Earlier, the NY Attorney General acted against 26 sweepstakes platforms, which stopped coin sales in NY. — [Sweepsy](https://www.sweepsy.com/news/maine-sweeps-ban-bill-hochul-new-york-law/)
- Bans were also enacted in Connecticut, Montana and New Jersey in 2025, and states escalated enforcement in 2026. Counts differ by tracker. — [iGB 2025 year in review](https://igamingbusiness.com/legal-compliance/2025-sweepstakes-casinos-year-in-review/); [Venable (May 2026)](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/05/states-escalate-crackdown-on-sweepstakes-casinos)
- Prophet Exchange left New Jersey in 2024 to relaunch as ProphetX, a sweepstakes sportsbook. It later moved to a CFTC-regulated prediction market. — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/prophet-exchange-to-relaunch-as-prophet-x-with-sweepstakes-sportsbook-game-july-30-2024); [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/166600/prophetx-gets-cftc-approval-to-operate-prediction-market)

### Inferences
- A sweepstakes overlay on a P2P market is especially fragile. The prize is funded by other users' losses (pooled value), the outcome turns on chance events, and AMOE adequacy is litigated. It is also exposed to class actions (loss-recovery suits) and to bans that reach vendors.

### Gaps
- No complete, authoritative state-by-state ban list was found. Trackers disagree.
- Specific 2025–2026 class-action outcomes against sweepstakes operators were not researched in depth.

## 5. Skill contest / tournament model

### Takeaway
Paid-entry skill contests with **fixed, pre-announced prizes not funded by entry fees** are lawful in many dominant-factor states. Strict "material element" or "any chance" states (Tennessee and South Carolina cited) and contest-specific laws carve out large parts of the country. Forecasting real-world events is arguably chance-heavy, so this is a weak fit for a P2P market and is best used only for limited tournaments.

### Cited Findings
- There are three tests: dominant factor (majority), material element, and any chance. — [Ifrah Law](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/gambling-on-skill-enforcement-of-gambling-laws-against-skill-games-in-outlier-states/)
- Tennessee treats even a trivial chance element as gambling. South Carolina case law treats paid-entry games as prohibited whether by skill or chance. — [Ifrah Law](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/gambling-on-skill-enforcement-of-gambling-laws-against-skill-games-in-outlier-states/); [firstamendment.com](https://www.firstamendment.com/skill-gaming-states/)
- Lawful structures typically require the prize to be set in advance and not pooled from entry fees. UIGEA's fantasy exemption has similar conditions. — [terms.law trading-contest matrix](https://terms.law/Trading-Legal/guides/trading-contest-state-matrix.html); [Lewis Roca](https://lewisroca.com/a-practical-guide-for-offering-online-contests-and-promotions-1)
- Vermont amended its law to permit entry fees for skill contests, which shows that state rules change. — [Olshan](https://www.olshanlaw.com/blogs-Advertising-Law-Blog,Vermont-Entry-Fee-Skill-Contest)

### Inferences
- An entry-fee forecasting tournament (for example, best Brier score over a season, with a fixed sponsor-funded prize) is more defensible than per-market P2P stakes. It would need geofencing of the strict states and legal review.
- A continuous P2P order book where the user pays and wins against other users is a bet or swap, not a contest.

### Gaps
- No verified current list of states prohibiting paid-entry skill contests was found (Arizona, Arkansas, Delaware, Louisiana and Montana were not confirmed).

## 6. State-licensed betting exchange (Sporttrade / Prophet model)

### Takeaway
State gaming licences allow P2P **sports** exchange wagering only, and only state by state. Sporttrade is licensed in NJ (DGE) and CO (Division of Gaming). Exchange wagering is "unavailable" in Iowa per its support page. Prophet Exchange launched in NJ in Aug 2022, exited in 2024, and moved to sweepstakes and then CFTC. This route cannot host user-created non-sports yes/no markets and is expensive.

### Cited Findings
- Sporttrade says it is licensed in New Jersey and Colorado and that exchange wagering is currently unavailable in Iowa. Earlier reports said it ran traditional betting only in Colorado and Iowa. — [Sporttrade support](https://sporttrade.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/42792922351259); [Vixio](https://www.vixio.com/insights/gc-startup-exchange-product-sporttrade-looks-attract-new-type-customers)
- Prophet Exchange launched 29 Aug 2022 as the first US P2P sports betting exchange (New Jersey), later went offline in New Jersey, and relaunched as ProphetX. — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220829005434/en/Prophet-Exchange-Launches-as-First-U.S.-Sports-Betting-Exchange-Giants-Legend-Victor-Cruz-Places-Inaugural-Bet); [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/183374/prophet-exchange-offline-in-nj-sports-betting-market/)
- Commentary argues that state-by-state regulation "breaks" the exchange model because liquidity is fragmented by state. — [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/?p=8781)

### Inferences
- The route usually needs a casino or skin partner (in NJ, online sports wagering runs through casino or racetrack licensees), a supplier or operator licence, GLI-certified technology, and per-state fees and taxes. Total cost likely runs well into seven figures. This is an inference; no reported total was found.

### Gaps
- No verified per-state licence fee or timeline for an exchange operator was found. Arizona and Virginia exchange status was unconfirmed.

## 7. Offshore incorporation with a US geoblock

### Takeaway
High personal risk for a US-person founder. US law follows US persons, and facilitating US access, including via VPN leakage, invites CFTC and DOJ action. Polymarket paid a $1.4M CFTC penalty in 2022, and the FBI searched the founder's home in Nov 2024 in a probe over US users bypassing geoblocks via VPN. That probe closed without charges in July 2025, a politically contingent outcome. Offshore licences are cheap (Anjouan about €17–30k, Curaçao about €19k–€47k per year) but do not protect against US or EU enforcement, and EU regulators have ISP-blocked Polymarket.

### Cited Findings
- In January 2022 the CFTC fined Polymarket $1.4M for offering event-based binary options to US users without a DCM or SEF registration. Polymarket then geoblocked the US. — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/polymarket-strikes-usd112-million-deal-to-return-to-the-united-states)
- The FBI searched CEO Shayne Coplan's apartment on about 13 Nov 2024 and seized devices. Probes examined US users bypassing controls via VPN. DOJ and CFTC closed the probes with declination notices around 15 Jul 2025, with no charges. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2025-07-15-us-justice-department-and-cftc-close-probes-into-polymarket-bloomberg-362703); [NBC](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/trump-administration-ends-polymarket-investigations-without-charges/3868886/); [Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/us-concludes-investigation-into-polymarket/)
- **Curaçao:** the master or sub-licence system ended, and the final master licence expired 31 Jan 2025. Operators now apply directly to the Curaçao Gaming Authority. Blog estimates put the application at about €4,592 and annual fees at about €47,450, or a minimum of ANG 35,000 (about $19k) per year; the figures conflict. — [Legarithm](https://legarithm.io/curacao-igaming-license-cost-process/); [Coincub](https://coincub.com/blog/curacao-gaming-license/); [CGA publication](https://portal.cga.cw/uploads/publications/aZPlS7pIPL5pJubskS3bNhtWjOXPNAawyRrFClRf.pdf)
- **Anjouan:** first-year cost is about €17.8k–€30k, with no GGR tax (vendor blogs). — [Legarithm](https://legarithm.io/anjouan-gaming-license-a-viable-alternative-to-curacao/); [Track360](https://track360.io/blog/gambling-license-cost-comparison-all-jurisdictions-2026)
- **Isle of Man (GSC):** application £5,250 and annual fee £36,750 for a full licence. Duty is 1.5% of GGR up to £20m, falling above that (blogs). — [Track360](https://www.track360.io/blog/online-gambling-license-jurisdictions-costs-operator-guide-2026); [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/gambling-license-costs-by-jurisdiction-data)
- **Malta (MGA):** annual fee is about €25k. First-year all-in estimates range from €60k–€100k up to €100k–€200k+ (blogs; conflicting). — [SourceCodeLab](https://sourcecodelab.co/real-cost-igaming-licensing/); [Track360](https://track360.io/blog/gambling-license-cost-comparison-all-jurisdictions-2026)

### Inferences
- An offshore gambling licence does not authorize offering US swaps or event contracts. A US founder still operating or controlling the business risks CEA violations and, depending on facts, state gambling and federal statutes (Wire Act, UIGEA). A geoblock helps but is not a safe harbor if VPN users are knowingly tolerated.
- Curaçao and Anjouan licences are not recognized in most EU and UK markets, where local licences are required.

### Gaps
- No primary-source fee schedules (CGA, Anjouan, GSC, MGA) were fetched. All figures are from consultant or vendor blogs.

## 8. Research and academic exceptions (IEM, PredictIt)

### Takeaway
Effectively unavailable to a new commercial founder. Iowa Electronic Markets (CFTC no-action letters 1992/1993) and PredictIt (Victoria University of Wellington, 2014 no-action letter) relied on university, non-profit, small-stake conditions. The CFTC withdrew PredictIt's letter in Aug 2022, and PredictIt survived only through litigation (5th Cir. 2023) and a July 2025 resolution.

## 9. UK: Gambling Act 2005 betting intermediary (exchange) licence, and EU overview

### Takeaway
The UK offers the cleanest real-money home for a P2P exchange with a commission-on-winnings model: a **Remote Betting Intermediary operating licence** (the Betfair, Smarkets and Matchbook model) from the Gambling Commission. Regulatory fees are modest:
- The general application fee rises from £185 to £231 from 1 Oct 2026. The fee table for the intermediary licence specifically is set by gross gambling yield (GGY) band.
- Annual fees are GGY-banded. The 2017 regulations had about £5,282 for the lowest remote intermediary band, a figure that is outdated, and a 2026 consultation proposes uplifts.

The real cost is compliance:
- UK entity and personal management licences
- AML, KYC and affordability checks
- Technical standards
- Remote gambling duty

Financial-event contracts may fall under FCA rules (spread bets or CFDs) instead. EU gambling is national. Polymarket has been blocked in France, Belgium, Poland, Switzerland and Singapore, and in July 2026 France ordered an ISP block.

### Cited Findings
- The Gambling Commission offers remote and non-remote **betting intermediary** operating licences. A licence is needed where gambling facilities are provided under s.5 of the Gambling Act 2005. — [GC: Remote betting intermediary licence](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/licences-and-fees/remote-betting-intermediary-operating-licence)
- The non-remote betting intermediary application fee is £285 for the lowest band (GGY under £5.5M). The first annual fee is due 30 days after issue, reduced by 25%. — [GC: Non-remote betting intermediary](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/licences-and-fees/non-remote-betting-intermediary-operating-licence)
- The GC "how much you'll pay" page says it costs £185 to apply, and from 1 Oct 2026 the fee will be £231. How this relates to the GGY-banded operating licence fee tables is unclear. — [GC: Application costs](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/guide/page/how-much-youll-pay)
- 2017 Fees Regulations (outdated): remote betting intermediary annual fees by GGY band started at **£5,282** for the lowest band. — [SI 2017/303](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/303/data.html)
- A DCMS consultation (annexes updated 29 Jan 2026) proposes changes to GC fees, including for betting intermediary and trading-rooms licences, with a 30% uplift scenario. — [GOV.UK consultation annexes](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/proposed-changes-to-gambling-commission-fees/proposed-changes-to-gambling-commission-fees-annexes)
- **Polymarket blocks:**
  - Switzerland (Nov 2024 blocklist)
  - Poland (late Nov 2024 register)
  - Singapore (ISP block on 8 Jan 2025)
  - Belgium (Gaming Commission blacklist on 12 Jan 2025)
  - France: the ANJ ordered ISPs to block on 16 Jul 2026, treating Polymarket as illegal gambling, with fines up to €100k.
  - About 30 regulators have acted since 2024, and Polymarket self-geoblocks about 36 regions.
  — [ANJ](https://anj.fr/promotion-illegal-gambling-services-blocking-polymarket-website); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/french-gambling-authority-blocks-polymarket); [iGB](https://igamingbusiness.com/prediction-markets/anj-orders-isps-block-polymarket-illegal-gambling-concerns/); [PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/legal/2026/polymarket-faces-ban-by-frances-gambling-regulator)
- **Malta (MGA) and Isle of Man costs:** see §7.

### Inferences
- **UK requirements, from general knowledge; verify against the GC Licence Conditions and Codes of Practice (LCCP):**
  - a personal management licence for key persons
  - source-of-funds evidence
  - segregation and disclosure of customer funds
  - AML and age verification
  - safer-gambling and affordability obligations
  - Remote Gambling Duty on gross profit, which for an exchange is commission
  - The Gambling Act's "gambling software" licence and testing may also apply.
- The UK model maps directly onto "platform takes no position, keeps a % of net winnings": UK exchanges historically charge commission on net market winnings.
- Realistic all-in first-year cost for a lean UK remote intermediary is plausibly six figures (£100k–£300k+) once legal, compliance, tech audit and staff are counted. Timeline is about 16 weeks or more for the application. **Both are estimates; not sourced.**
- **Financial-event markets:** bets on prices, indices or rates may be regulated investments (CFDs or spread bets) under FCA rules rather than gambling. Non-financial events (politics, sports, culture) fall under the Gambling Commission.
- **EU:** gambling licensing is national; there is no EU passport. Financial-event contracts could fall under MiFID II as derivatives, and crypto-settled models raise MiCA issues. An EU launch means per-country licences (or an MGA licence where Malta's licence is accepted) and blocking elsewhere.

- The current remote betting intermediary application and annual fee tables were not retrieved: the GC site was blocked from fetch. The status of the 2026 fee changes is unconfirmed.
- No source was retrieved on GC processing times, FCA treatment of event contracts, or MiFID and MiCA classification of event contracts.
- Smarkets, Betfair and Matchbook commission rates and licence numbers were not retrieved.

## 10. Summary: cheapest realistic legal path and real-money cost and time

### Takeaway
**Cheapest and lowest liability:** keep the product play-money (Manifold model): no purchasable currency redeemable for value, no prizes. Monetize via ads, subscriptions or B2B. Optionally add fixed-prize, sponsor-funded forecasting tournaments, geofenced from strict states. **Cheapest US real-money path:** become a guaranteed IB or white-label distributor for an existing DCM (Kalshi or Polymarket US). **Cheapest jurisdiction for owning a true P2P exchange:** a UK GC remote betting intermediary licence, which is small in regulatory fees but needs real compliance spend. **Owning a US DCM or DCO** costs millions and years, or tens to hundreds of millions to acquire. Avoid sweepstakes and offshore-with-geoblock as a US person.

### Cited Findings (cross-references)
- DCM: 12–24+ months, $5M+ financial resources (KPMG estimate). — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/prediction-markets-path-entry.html)
- Acquisition comparables: QCEX $112M (2025); LedgerX $50M (2023). — [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/polymarket-qcex-acquisition); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/robinhood-prediction-markets-ftx-linked-acquisition-report)
- Independent IB: $45k–$50k adjusted net capital; a guaranteed IB is exempt via an FCM guarantee. — [NFA](https://www.nfa.futures.org/members/ib/regulatory-obligations/guaranteed-ib-requirements.html)
- Sweepstakes bans in CA, NY, CT, MT, NJ and others. — [iGB](https://igamingbusiness.com/legal-compliance/2025-sweepstakes-casinos-year-in-review/)
- UK GC fees are in the hundreds to low thousands of pounds. — [GC](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/guide/page/how-much-youll-pay)

### Inferences
Rough cost and time ladder. These are estimates unless cited above.

| Path | Cost | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Play-money | ~$0 regulatory; lawyer review ~$5k–$25k | weeks |
| Fixed-prize tournaments | legal review plus geofencing, low five figures | 1–3 months |
| Guaranteed IB on a DCM | low-to-mid six figures | 3–9 months, plus partner negotiation |
| UK remote betting intermediary | £100k–£300k+ first year | 6–12 months |
| Offshore (Curaçao/Anjouan) | €20k–€50k/yr | a few months; high US-person legal risk, EU blocks |
| State sports exchange | seven figures | 1–2+ years per state; sports only |
| De novo DCM (+DCO) | $5M–$10M+ | 1–2+ years |
| Acquire a DCM | $50M–$112M+ (reported comparables) | months, plus CFTC change-of-control review |

- Liability minimization ranking, best first: play-money; partner (IB / front end); UK licence; DCM; state exchange; offshore; sweepstakes.

### Gaps
- No authoritative published cost of compliance for a small IB or UK intermediary was found. The ladder's non-cited numbers are estimates for the report writer to flag as such.
